## What we are actually trying to capture

Humor is three separate things, and a pattern file has to hold all three:

1. **Generation:** what they say when they are being funny.
2. **Reception:** what they find funny when someone else says it. Often not the same
   as what they generate. A deadpan person can love slapstick.
3. **Occasions:** when humor shows up at all. Some people joke when nervous, some when
   bored, some only with people they trust. This is the part outsiders get wrong most.

The scenario battery in `calibration/humor-round-01.md` tests all three.

## Feature vocabulary

To describe the mechanism without adjectives, use these named mechanisms. Tick the ones
that show up in samples; the pattern is the _weighted combination_, not a single label.

| Mechanism        | What it looks like                                       |
| :--------------- | :------------------------------------------------------- |
| Incongruity      | Two things that do not belong together put together      |
| Exaggeration     | Scaling a real thing up until it breaks                  |
| Understatement   | Describing a big thing as small, flatly                  |
| Literalism       | Taking a figure of speech or request at face value       |
| Wordplay         | Puns, sound-alikes, double meanings, cross-language puns |
| Callback         | Referencing an earlier joke or shared moment             |
| Absurdism        | Internally consistent nonsense                           |
| Deadpan          | Saying the joke with no signal that it is a joke         |
| Self-deprecation | The person is the target                                 |
| Roast            | A present person is the target, affectionately           |
| Observational    | Pointing at something everyone sees but nobody says      |
| Dark             | Death, failure, disaster treated lightly                 |
| Sarcasm          | Saying the opposite with tone carrying the meaning       |
| Meta             | Joking about the joke, the conversation, or the medium   |
| Escalation       | Taking someone else's joke one step further              |

Also record **target** (self, friends, strangers, institutions, situations, ideas, the
model) and **register** (dry, expressive, performative, muttered).
